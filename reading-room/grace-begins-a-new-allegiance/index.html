Foundations · 6 minute read

Grace begins a new allegiance.

The Gospel is not a way to avoid the truth about sin. It is God’s decisive way of bringing people home through Christ.

The good news begins with God, not with our effort. We were made for communion with Him, but sin distorts worship, desire, relationship and the use of power. It does not only produce individual mistakes; it bends people and communities away from the life they were created to bear. We cannot repair that separation through performance, moral comparison or religious activity.

In Jesus, God comes near. Christ reveals the Father, bears sin’s cost, defeats death and opens the way of reconciliation. This is mercy with substance. It is not permission to remain unchanged; it is the beginning of a new life in which forgiveness, repentance, belonging and obedience become possible by grace.

Faith is more than agreement

To trust Christ is to receive what we could not achieve and to let that gift change our direction. We learn to confess honestly, turn from what destroys life, receive the Spirit’s help and join a people who are also being formed. This is not self-salvation. It is the fruit of being welcomed into a Kingdom that does not belong to us.

When shame says, “Hide,” grace calls us into the light. When fear says, “Secure yourself,” grace teaches us to trust. When ambition says, “Make your name,” grace gives us a better centre: the love and lordship of Jesus.

For a first conversation

Read Romans 5:6–11. Explain the Gospel in your own words without religious shortcuts. What does Christ’s reconciliation make possible today?

Continue your formation

Read slowly. Practise faithfully.
